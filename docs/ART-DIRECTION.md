# Art direction

## Ownership statement

**FINAL ART ASSETS ARE EXTERNAL APPROVED INPUTS.** Codex integrates approved assets but does not redefine their design. Approved visual assets and reference files are immutable unless the user explicitly requests modification.

No final or placeholder art is created in Stage 0.

## Visual tone

- Friendly, modern, colourful, and calm rather than visually noisy.
- Soft rounded shapes and clear silhouettes suitable for ages 4–6.
- Warm optimism and gentle curiosity rather than high-energy arcade pressure.
- Portfolio-quality illustration with consistent lighting, perspective, edge treatment, and material language.
- High contrast between interactive objects and the background without harsh neon.

Avoid a generic cheap mobile-game appearance, excessive gradients, neon cyberpunk styling, photorealism, frightening expressions, cluttered backgrounds, and tiny decoration that competes with play.

## UI philosophy

- One dominant action or learning focus at a time.
- Large interactive targets and generous negative space.
- Minimal text; favor recognizable symbols plus optional voice.
- Clear visual hierarchy: task → answer choices → progress → secondary controls.
- Sound and replay controls remain easy to find without dominating.
- Correct/incorrect meaning is never conveyed by colour alone.
- UI remains legible at 320 px width and inside mobile safe areas.

## Robot character requirements

- Friendly, expressive, non-realistic robot with a readable face at small sizes.
- Broken state appears in need of help, never injured, frightening, or distressed.
- Modular construction must support five visually meaningful repair increments.
- Component boundaries, pivots, overlap order, and attachment points must be specified consistently.
- Expressions and poses cover broken/inactive, idle, repair reaction, happy, and victory.
- The complete state must be compositionally consistent with the modular build.

## Background requirements

- A welcoming laboratory setting with simple rounded forms and a restrained detail level.
- Start and main-game compositions may be distinct but must clearly belong to one environment.
- Preserve quiet zones for the robot, task objects, progress, and controls in portrait and landscape crops.
- Background contrast and saturation remain below interactive objects.
- Important details may not sit in crop-sensitive outer edges.

## Task object requirements

- Objects require clean, unmistakable silhouettes and age-appropriate familiarity.
- Within a question, scale, perspective, outline, and rendering style remain consistent.
- Category distinctions must be semantically clear, not based on tiny details.
- Shadow-task silhouettes must remain recognizable without internal detail.
- Memory items need strong shape differentiation and stable visual identity.
- Transparent padding and pivot conventions must be consistent across a set.

## Animation requirements

- Motion should clarify state changes, direct attention, and reward progress.
- Use readable anticipation and settle, gentle squash/stretch where appropriate, and restrained particles.
- Incorrect feedback is subtle and non-punitive.
- Repair transitions clearly connect task completion to one restored module.
- Victory is joyful but short enough to preserve the 4–7 minute session.
- Provide reduced-motion variants by shortening or replacing nonessential motion.

## Consistency rules

- Maintain one approved palette, outline/edge system, lighting direction, shadow softness, and perspective.
- Maintain documented canvas bounds, pivots, naming, export scale, and transparency rules.
- Do not mix illustration sources or stylistic generations.
- Review all assets together at desktop, tablet, and mobile presentation sizes before approval.
- Any approved optimization must preserve source files and visual fidelity.
